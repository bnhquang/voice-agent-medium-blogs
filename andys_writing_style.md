# Andy's writing style

How I write and talk, worked out from my own chats with Claude Code on this laptop, so drafts (Medium posts first) can sound like me.

**Source:** 3,352 of my messages across every Claude Code project on this machine, 22 Jun to 3 Nov 2026 (mostly work: the voice agent, HubSpot tools, direct debit; a few personal). Pasted logs, emails and transcripts were taken out, and so were the reply buttons Claude writes for me, as far as they could be told apart. Also used: every time I asked for a draft to be reworded, and the feedback Claude saved about how I like things.

## Two modes

I write in two clearly different ways:

1. **Typed** (most messages). Fast, lowercase, short, slangy, typos left in.
2. **Dictated** (Wispr Flow). Full sentences with capitals and commas, longer, me thinking out loud. Wispr Flow tidies the grammar, so this is closest to how I actually speak.

An article should sound like mode 2, cleaned up: the way I'd explain it out loud to a mate who also builds things.

## Typed mode (how I chat)

- **Short.** Half my messages are 19 words or fewer. The ask comes first, then the reason if there is one.
- **Lowercase.** 79% start with a lowercase letter, "i" is usually lowercase, and 65% end with no punctuation at all.
- **Almost no exclamation marks** (0.3% of messages). Surprise or annoyance shows up as "???" instead ("why model takes too long???").
- **Aussie and casual.** "bro" (in about 200 messages), "yea", "nah", "bruh", "mate", "man", "yo", "reckon", "lol".
- **Shortcuts.** u, ur, sth, wanna, gonna, needa, prolly, atm, idk, yk, ykwim, "thingy", "and stuff", "or sth".
- **Typos stay in** when I'm moving fast ("currnet", "plesae"). Meaning over polish.
- **Openers I reach for:** "can you", "go ahead", "can we", "ok now", "give me", "wait what", "wait so", "hmm", "i want", "how do", "is there".
- **Thinking mid-message.** "i'm just thinking maybe...", "hmm or we can...", "i suppose", "not sure if this is the best phrasing".
- **Ask with the reason attached.** "deploy to dev bro anytime i ask for a change always do how else do i see the change".
- **Asking for a call, not a menu.** "what's the best approach based on what u know", "what u reckon?", "what's the best SaaS phrasing?"
- **Blunt when something's off.** "the hell is application submitted", "bruh what the hell it's recurrent???", "i swear the offset problem is still there".

## Dictated mode (how I talk)

- **Conversational openers:** "Okay, mate. The thing is, ...", "Honest question: ...", "Yo, like my machine, ..."
- **Tag questions to check we're on the same page:** "right?" (in 75 messages). "It's quite clear that he said yes and the timing is interesting. The agent has to be able to infer those sorts of things, right?"
- **Hedges and fillers:** "like", "sort of", "kind of", "I mean", "in the sense that", "or something like that", "big time".
- **Concrete examples and analogies to explain an idea.** "you know how in ChatGPT when you create a chat, the URL will be changed with a query parameter of the chat ID? I want to do exactly that." Or: say "tryna do" casually and it types "trying to do".
- **Scenario thinking.** "Imagine in 6 months' time: what course of action will we need to take now in order for us to not get into any legal issues?"
- **Strong, plain value words:** simple ("It's very simple"), elegant, seamless, foolproof, consistent, sustainable long-term, "not over the top", human and natural ("That's a thing that's not very human").
- **Honest about what I don't know.** "I honestly don't know which one's better", "I'm not sure if this is going to help the model... but".
- **Real enthusiasm, said straight.** "I'm honestly very, very impressed with Wispr Flow's transcription."

## How I want writing to read (from my own edits)

These come straight from what I asked to change in drafts:

- **No em dashes. Ever.** Asked again and again ("remove em dash", "remove all m dashes on the website"), now a global rule. Use commas, colons, brackets or a new sentence.
- **Short and to the point.** "shorter more to the point", "shorter bruh just 3 bullets", "more succint please", "too verbose just say sms sent", "don't be verbose just keep it extra simple".
- **Plain text over formatting.** "give me plain text email please", "make it with no table please plain text". No table or bullets unless they really help.
- **Formal but simple** for anything external: "make the language formal but simpler man".
- **Explain simply, no jargon.** "can you explain simply what ... is", "i don't really get what promptfoo do can you explain simply i just dont see its value".
- **Show, don't just report.** I judge a change by reading what actually happened (real transcripts, simulated calls, a quick HTML mock), not by a table of numbers. "visualise it to me using actual html and i will see if i like it."
- **Lean.** No captions that restate the obvious, no two numbers saying the same thing, nothing that "doesn't do anything".
- **Specific, not generic.** "if we just say studio it sounds generic, i want something specific."
- **Labels and wording matter.** I'll stop on one word that's wrong ("the hell is application submitted", "this footnote is wrong though should phrase it some other way").

## How I write to other people

Short, friendly, a bit self-aware, straight to the point, ends warm. A LinkedIn note I wrote to a founder whose product was down:

> Hi hope you're well. A little weird of a way to reach out but I think your server has been inaccessible for several hours now, so I thought I'd bring it to attention. Love the product though.

## For Medium articles

Take dictated mode and clean it up the way Wispr Flow would: keep the voice, lose the filler.

**Do**
- Write in first person, as someone who built the thing and measured it.
- Open with the problem in one or two plain sentences, the way I'd say it out loud ("The thing is, the caller only remembers the slowest turn").
- Use short sentences and short paragraphs. One idea each.
- Explain each term once, simply, with an everyday analogy (like the ChatGPT chat-ID one).
- Use real numbers I measured and real (anonymised) call snippets. Show what the agent actually said.
- Say what didn't work and what I'm not sure about. "I honestly don't know which one's better" is fine.
- Give my actual opinion and recommendation, plainly.
- Keep a light Aussie, casual feel: "a fair bit", "pretty good", the odd "mate" at most. No "bro" or text shortcuts.
- End each section with the takeaway in one line.

**Don't**
- Em dashes.
- Long intros, throat-clearing, or restating the title.
- Corporate or hype words ("leverage", "game-changer", "unlock", "in today's fast-paced world") and anything that sounds AI-written.
- Tables or bullet lists where two sentences would do.
- Jargon nobody explains.
- Exclamation marks.
- Padding a point that's already made.

**Before publishing, check:** no em dashes; every paragraph earns its place; every term is explained; there is at least one real example or number per section; it reads well out loud.
